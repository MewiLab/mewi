Let me steelman your friend first, because the argument is good in general.
Your friend's argument is correct — in general
The principle is sound: Unity is sitting right there with the navmesh, the transforms, the colliders. Computing "which reachable zone has the lowest visit count" is a trivial loop over a few dozen zones — microseconds, every frame if you wanted. Sending that decision across a websocket to Python and back adds latency and a network dependency to answer a question Unity could answer instantly with data it already holds. As a general rule, don't put compute on the far side of a network boundary when the data and the cheap processor are both on the near side. Your friend is teaching you a good instinct. In a normal real-time agent — continuous control, steering every frame — they'd be unambiguously right and I'd side with them.
But the deciding question isn't "who can compute it" — it's "who already has the data, and when is the decision made"
Here's where your case diverges. The node-scoring computation is trivially cheap on either side — that's not the deciding factor, and that's the part of your friend's framing I'd gently correct. "Unity is strong enough to calculate it" is true but it's answering the wrong question. The thing that actually costs you is not the calculation; it's keeping the data the calculation needs in sync with the place it's calculated.
And the data this calculation needs — the visit counts, recency, novelty — is memory. It's exactly the per-cat state you already told me lives in Redis and persists to Supabase. Your own words: the backend is for long-term data and the agent. The visit-count overlay is long-term-ish agent data. So the real question is: do you want the scoring to happen where the data already lives, or do you want to continuously ship that data to Unity so Unity can score, then ship the result back?
If scoring lives in Unity, then on every tick you must either (a) push the cat's visit-count overlay up to the backend anyway so it can persist and so the LLM can read it for intent, and keep a copy in Unity to score against — now you have the same data in two places and a sync problem — or (b) make the overlay Unity-authoritative and constantly mirror it down to the backend. Either way you've created two owners of one piece of state, which is the single most reliable way to create bugs that are miserable to debug. Is the cat's "I've been to the dock 4 times" count in Unity right, or the one in Redis? When they disagree at 2am during your demo, which do you trust?
If scoring lives on the backend, there's exactly one owner. Unity reports a fact it's authoritative about — "cat arrived at zone Z" — and the backend, which owns all agent memory, increments, scores, and hands back a zone id. Unity never holds visit counts at all. One owner, no sync, no drift.
So my answer to "which is simpler to maintain": backend — because of where the data lives, not where the compute is
You asked which is simpler to maintain, and that's the right question to optimize for as a solo dev. Simpler-to-maintain is almost never about CPU cycles; it's about how many places a piece of truth lives. The backend answer gives you one owner for all agent memory, which is the thing you'll be debugging and tuning for months. Your friend's answer gives you a faster computation you didn't need to speed up, at the cost of a second copy of state you'll have to keep honest.
There's a clean way to express the boundary that honors both of you, and it's the rule I'd actually write down:
Unity owns the geometry; the backend owns the statistics. Unity is authoritative for everything physical and frame-rate: where zones are, what's reachable from here, am I currently in zone Z, the actual steering. That genuinely must be local and your friend is 100% right that it should never go to the network — and it doesn't. The backend is authoritative for everything that is memory: how many times this cat has visited Z, how long ago, how novel it is, what intent that implies. The scoring is a function of the statistics, so it lives with the statistics. The two meet at the tick you're already sending anyway — there's no extra round-trip, because reporting the arrival and receiving the next destination happen on the same turn-boundary message you already exchange.
That last point defuses the latency objection entirely: you are not adding a network hop. Your architecture is turn-based and gated on queue completion — the cat already stops and talks to the backend at exactly the moment a "where next" decision is needed. The decision rides on a message that exists regardless. Latency cost: zero. This is the specific reason the general principle ("don't cross the network to compute") doesn't bite here — the network crossing is already happening at precisely the decision point.
So concretely, the flow your friend and you can both be happy with:
Unity (geometry, authoritative):  "cat arrived at zone Z"   ──tick──▶  backend
Backend (statistics, authoritative):  overlay[Z].visits++  
                                        Z* = argmin(visits, recency-decayed, reachable*)
                                        persist to Redis (and Supabase if salient)
                                  ◀──reply──  "go to zone Z*"
Unity (geometry):  resolve Z* → centroid → enqueue travel actions → steer locally
reachable* has one subtlety: the backend scores over zones, but reachability is geometry Unity owns. Cleanest fix — Unity includes the current reachable-zone set (just a list of zone ids, tiny) in the arrival tick. Backend scores only within that set. Geometry stays Unity's, statistics stay backend's, and the boundary holds with no shared ownership.
To put a number on the thing your friend is worried about: the payload added to a message you're already sending is a short list of zone ids and a coverage summary — on the order of tens of bytes to a couple hundred. That is nothing. The maintenance cost of two state owners is, over the life of your project, enormously larger than that. For a solo dev optimizing for sanity, single-ownership wins decisively.
One honest caveat so I'm not overselling it: the day you ever want a cat to make a fast spatial reflex mid-journey — dodge, react to the player appearing right next to it, abort toward something — that reflex must be Unity-local and cannot wait for a tick. But that's your Reflex layer, which you already separate, and it's about interrupting travel, not choosing exploration targets. Exploration-target selection is a turn-boundary decision and belongs on the backend. Keep those two distinct and you and your friend are both right about your respective halves.
So: backend owns the overlay and the scoring, Unity sends arrival + reachable-zone-ids on the tick it already sends, and you carry zero extra round-trips.
